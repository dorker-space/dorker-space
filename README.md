# dorker.space

Looking for a job shouldn't become another full-time job.

Dorker started from my own frustration with searching across job boards, reviewing supposedly relevant roles, discovering hiring restrictions somewhere at the bottom, and rewriting the same experience for every application.

I'm building a tool to make that process less exhausting, while keeping the application decision with the person who will actually do the job.

## What Dorker does

Dorker brings job discovery, matching, discussion, and application preparation into one workflow.

The goal is to understand more than whether a job description contains technologies from your CV. What experience transfers to a different stack? Is a requirement essential or something you're comfortable learning? Can the company actually hire you where you live? Does the work fit what you want to do next?

The workflow being developed lets you:

- Browse jobs evaluated against your experience and preferences.
- Inspect the reasoning behind a recommendation and discuss uncertainties.
- Organize supporting evidence from your background.
- Prepare and edit application drafts using relevant personal materials.
- Decide what to send and where to apply.

## Around this organization

Dorker is maintained across repositories covering:

- **Backend and intelligence:** job ingestion, persistence, matching, discussions, and application generation.
- **Application frontend:** the interface for reviewing jobs, managing evidence, and preparing applications.
- **Website:** an introduction to the product and its development.

Each repository documents its own setup, dependencies, and current limitations.

## Current stage

Dorker is under active development. It began as a system for my own job search, and I'm preparing it for testing with other people.

Some parts already work; others are still being connected and evaluated. The project has not yet established that it improves hiring outcomes. That is something to test, rather than promise.
